\authoredsubsection{Sprint 2}{Gáspár}{Gáspár Harmati}\label{sec:sprint2}

Sprint 2 ran from 12 to 25 May 2026, with the review and retrospective on 26 May. Sprint 1 had produced the architectural foundation; this sprint turned it into the first working code. Gáspár Harmati took the Scrum Master role.

\subsubsection{Sprint Goals}\label{sec:sprint2-goals}

The goal was that the application routes queries to eight specialized agents, that traces are persisted to a database, that a first synthetic dataset exists, and that the judge and the optimizer are implemented and connected to the router. Each component either exists in the repository or does not, which made the goal measurable within the two-week sprint \autocite{schwaber2020scrum}.

\subsubsection{Sprint Planning}\label{sec:sprint2-planning}

Seven tickets totaling 25 story points matched the velocity of Sprint 1: the agents and their system prompts and the batch-evaluation wiring with Sebastian Straub, the synthetic dataset with Gáspár Harmati, the agent prompt design with Mohsin Saeed, the dispatcher and trace database with Julius Bethge, the judge with Marco Schröder, and the optimizer MVP with Gáspár Harmati and Mohsin Saeed jointly. Responding to Sprint 1 feedback, the team added a fixed in-person session on Tuesdays and kept two online stand-ups per week.

\subsubsection{Implementation and Execution}\label{sec:sprint2-execution}

The eight-agent roster was defined, initially with a PropertyLawAgent in the eighth slot that was only later settled in favor of the PlatformSupportAgent (\cref{sec:agent-architecture}), and the first synthetic dataset was calibrated against an internal Interhyp frequency reference with its tail imbalance softened so that rare agents remain scorable (\cref{sec:dataset-stages}). Agent prompts were given a uniform five-part structure to sharpen the boundaries between overlapping agents.

The dispatcher was built as the two-stage architecture the router still uses, at this point on a smaller embedding model with a single global threshold. The judge was implemented as a panel of independent models \autocite{verga2024juries,li_llms-as-judges_2024}, later reduced to a single judge (\cref{sec:judge-bias}), and the optimizer MVP implemented OPRO \autocite{yang2023llmoptimizers} against a mocked judge so that both could be developed in parallel, still rewriting the judge prompt rather than the routing prompt it targets today (\cref{sec:optimizer}).

\subsubsection{Sprint Review}\label{sec:sprint2-review}

At the review on 26 May, Interhyp confirmed that the domain analogy and the dataset calibration matched its setup closely enough and asked for a dashboard presenting routing accuracy against the secondary KPIs as a single accuracy-performance frontier. Interhyp also cautioned against scope creep, arguing that a smaller set of components finished with measurable results was worth more than a larger partially working system.

All 25 story points were delivered, but the sprint showed the same late-finishing pattern as Sprint 1, with the three five-point tickets closing in the final days. The cumulative flow diagram shows this as a persistent band of in-progress work (\cref{fig:sprint2-cumulative-flow}); its spike on 26 May is backlog grooming for Sprint 3.

\subsubsection{Sprint Retrospective}\label{sec:sprint2-retrospective}

The retrospective mapped sprint events by impact and frequency, revisited the two process concerns raised by the Sprint 1 reviewer, and closed with a Circle of Influence step separating what the team controls from what it can only influence. Two problems were confirmed. The gap between the Tuesday and Thursday meetings was too wide once the larger tickets became interdependent, so an optional Friday stand-up was trialed. The three five-point tickets each hid several sub-deliverables, so the team agreed to split anything above three points at planning and to add a mid-sprint refinement session, a rule that visibly shaped the Sprint 3 backlog (\cref{sec:sprint3-planning}). The overriding action was to integrate router, judge, and optimizer into a single end-to-end run.
